# Stability-aware channel, band and window selection for EEG visual imagery: final report

Project team R01 / R02 / R07, 2026-11-10. Every number in this report is taken from
`docs/RESULTS.md` (pre-registered analysis), `docs/P5_REPORT.md` (exploratory
checks), `docs/DATA_NOTES.md` (data), or the tables and figures in `results/final/`,
which are regenerated by `python -m reports.make_final`. Table and figure numbers
(T1–T6, F01–F11) refer to `results/final/tables/TABLES.md` and
`results/final/figures/CAPTIONS.md`.

## Abstract

We asked whether a stability-aware multi-objective genetic algorithm (NSGA-II),
which selects EEG channels, frequency bands and time windows for a filter-bank CSP +
shrinkage-LDA classifier, decodes imagined shapes (circle, square, pentagram; chance
1/3) better than the same classifier on all components. Data: 22 subjects (42
sessions) of a public visual-imagery dataset. The primary hypothesis and analysis
were pre-registered before any result was analysed. **The primary hypothesis is not
supported:** in leave-one-block-out cross-validation, the GA reached 0.345 balanced
accuracy against 0.353 for the full baseline (Δ −0.007, one-sided p = 0.79, n = 22).
No pre-registered secondary comparison was significant: against an accuracy-only GA,
the cross-session baseline, or EEGNet with 32 or 8 channels (all p_Holm = 1.000).
Every method is within 0.012 BA of the others and no more than 0.02 above chance.
The GA reached this accuracy at about 30 % of the cost (13 of 168 features), but
exploratory controls show the selection itself carries no information. Random
subsets of the same size score the same (Δ +0.004, n.s.). Selections are as unstable
as random (Kuncheva 0.08), and the GA's inner-CV accuracy is inflated by +0.17 by
selection. All models fit their training data (training BA 0.58–1.00) and generalise
to near chance. On the viewing period, where the signal is decodable (0.435), the GA
loses a significant 0.025 BA to the full baseline. The imagery period of this
dataset carries too little transferable class information for component selection
to help.

## 1. Introduction

Mental-imagery EEG decoding is limited by a weak, subject-specific signal and by
small numbers of trials. A common response is to restrict the model to a few
informative components, which is cheaper to compute and to record and, it is hoped,
less prone to overfitting. Genetic algorithms are a standard tool for that
selection. This project tested a variant that also rewards **stability** across
inner folds, and picks the cheapest solution within one standard deviation of the
best inner accuracy. The question was whether such a selection beats the unselected
pipeline under a strict evaluation: leave-one-block-out within session and
train-on-session-1/test-on-session-2 across sessions, with every selection nested
inside the training data.

The analysis plan (`docs/ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`) was committed and pushed before the
group results were analysed. Its primary and secondary tests are reported first
(§5.1–5.2). Everything else is labelled exploratory (§5.3).

## 2. Data

Gao et al., *Visual Imagery* dataset (figshare DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.30227503),
figure task (`docs/PROJECT.md`, `docs/DATA_NOTES.md`):

* 22 subjects; sub-09 and sub-10 have only session 1, so there are **42 recordings**
  and 20 subjects for cross-session.
* 32 EEG channels at 1000 Hz (Neuracle, reference CPz); 120 trials per session (40
  per shape) in 4 blocks of 30.
* Trial timeline: fixation 0–3 s, **viewing 3–7 s**, mask 7–9 s, **imagery 9–13 s**,
  rest 13–17 s. The single event marker is the imagery onset.
* Preprocessing: notch at 50 Hz and harmonics, 1–40 Hz band-pass, common average
  reference, resampling to 250 Hz, imagery epoch of 4 s from the marker. Trials with
  a peak-to-peak amplitude above 150 µV on the 8 candidate channels are rejected:
  **126 of 5040 trials (2.5 %)**, leaving 4914; the smallest class in any recording
  has 33 trials.
* Trial order: within a block, 10 trials per class in a fixed pseudo-random order.
  Only 19 distinct 120-trial sequences are used across the 42 recordings. sub-01
  repeats its sequence across sessions, and (found in P5) 30-trial block orders are
  reused across sessions for 10 of 20 subjects (DATA_NOTES §4 and P5 addendum).

## 3. Methods

All methods use the same cached epochs and the same outer splits (`docs/PROJECT.md`,
`docs/CONTRACTS.md`, frozen at tag `p1-integrated`, config hash `08e124167b`).

* **Components.** 8 parietal-occipital candidate channels (O1, Oz, O2, Pz, P3, P4, P7,
  P8), 4 bands (theta 4–7, alpha 8–12, beta 13–30, low gamma 30–40 Hz), 7 windows of
  1 s starting every 0.5 s (0–3 s). A chromosome is a 19-bit mask (at least 2
  channels, 1 band and 1 window). Cost = (n_ch/8 + n_bands/4 + n_win/7)/3.
* **Classifier.** One-vs-rest FBCSP (one filter pair per class per band × window) and
  shrinkage LDA.
* **`fbcsp_all` (baseline 1).** All 19 genes on: 168 features.
* **`ga_nsga` (proposed).** NSGA-II (pymoo), population 30, 20 generations,
  objectives: inner-CV BA (max), inner-CV SD (min), cost (min). Inner CV is 3-fold
  over the 3 training blocks. Final choice: the cheapest Pareto entry with inner BA
  within 1 SD of the best. Seeds 0, 1, 2.
* **`ga_single` (baseline 2).** The same GA on inner-CV BA only.
* **`eegnet_32ch` / `eegnet_8ch` (comparison).** EEGNet (braindecode) on the
  1–40 Hz epoch, all 32 channels or the 8 candidate channels. Early stopping on a
  stratified 20 % validation split of the training fold, best weights restored.
  Seeds 0, 1, 2.

## 4. Evaluation protocol

* **Block protocol (within session):** leave-one-block-out, 4 folds per recording.
  The GA sees only the 3 training blocks.
* **Cross-session:** train on session 1, test on session 2 (20 subjects).
* **Unit of analysis:** one score per subject, with seeds averaged and then sessions
  (n = 22 block, n = 20 cross-session). Balanced accuracy; chance = 1/3. SDs are
  sample SDs over subjects.
* **Pre-registered tests** (`docs/ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`): primary `ga_nsga` >
  `fbcsp_all`, block, one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank, α = 0.05. Secondary family of
  four (S1 vs `ga_single`, S2 cross-session vs `fbcsp_all`, S3 vs `eegnet_32ch`,
  S4 vs `eegnet_8ch`): two-sided Wilcoxon, Holm-corrected. Per-recording
  above-chance tests: label permutation (1000 permutations), Holm over recordings.
* **Cross-session drop:** session-2 block BA minus cross-session BA.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 Pre-registered primary hypothesis (T2, F01)

| n | Mean Δ (GA-NSGA − FBCSP all) | Median Δ | One-sided p | r_rb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 22 | −0.007 | −0.004 | 0.788 | −0.19 |

**Not supported.** The realised SD of the paired differences (0.045) gives a minimum
detectable difference of about 0.024 BA, so there is no evidence of a gain of that
size. The exploratory 95 % t-interval (−0.027 to +0.013) also leaves a loss of up to
about 0.03 open (RESULTS.md §2).

| S3 GA-NSGA vs EEGNet (32 ch) | block | 22 | +0.004 | 0.975 | 1.000 |
| S4 GA-NSGA vs EEGNet (8 ch) | block | 22 | −0.002 | 1.000 | 1.000 |

| Method | Block BA | Cross-session BA | Drop | Recordings > chance (Holm, of 42) | Cost | Features / params |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FBCSP all | 0.353 ± 0.044 | 0.337 ± 0.039 | +0.027 | 2 | 1.00 | 168 |

* All methods are within 0.012 BA of each other on block, and none is more than
  0.02 above chance. Only 1–3 of 42 recordings per method survive Holm correction.
* GA-NSGA selects 2.7 channels, 1.3 bands and 1.6 windows on average. Selection is
  nearly flat (18–43 % per gene; most frequent Pz 43 %, then Oz 38 %; F04), and
  selection stability is near chance (Kuncheva 0.078, no higher than `ga_single`'s
  0.084; T3).
* The GA's inner-CV accuracy for its chosen chromosome exceeds the outer test
  accuracy by +0.17 (`ga_nsga`) and +0.21 (`ga_single`) (RESULTS.md §4.4).
* GA-NSGA has the smallest cross-session drop (+0.002), but this comes from its lower
  block accuracy, not from a higher cross-session accuracy (S2 n.s.).

### 5.3 Exploratory checks (P5; T4–T6, F05–F11)

None of these were in the analysis plan.

1. **Trial-position confound** (P5 §1, F08). A classifier that sees only a trial's
   position in its block predicts the class cross-session (0.397, p < 0.001; 0.390
   without sub-01), because block orders recur across sessions. The EEG methods do
   not follow it: no gain on the 10 subjects with shared blocks (FBCSP all 0.337 vs
   0.337), and trial-level agreement κ ≈ 0. For sub-01, position and class are
   perfectly confounded, so its cross-session 0.406 cannot be interpreted.
2. **Random chromosomes at matched cost** (P5 §2, F06). The GA's chosen subset is
   indistinguishable from a random subset with the same numbers of channels, bands
   and windows: Δ +0.004 (GA-NSGA, p = 0.42) and +0.007 (GA single-obj., p = 0.20).
   Its mean percentile in the random distribution is 0.51 / 0.52. **The search adds
   nothing beyond the cost reduction.**
3. **Chooser rule (§k12)** (P5 §3, F07). Seven rules applied to the stored fronts
   (best inner BA, 0.5/1/2 SD, SE, knee, minimum cost) give 0.338–0.351. None differs
   from the full baseline or from the contract rule. These are test-set scores, so
   they cannot be used to pick a rule.
4. **Random-split inflation** (P5 §4.1, F09). Random 5-fold splits do not inflate any
   method (−0.002 to −0.011, all n.s.).
5. **Viewing period** (P5 §4.2, T6, F11). The perception period decodes well above
   imagery (FBCSP all 0.435, +0.083; 12–20 recordings significant after Holm). There,
   GA-NSGA is significantly below FBCSP all (0.410, Δ −0.025, p = 0.004) and below
   GA single-obj. (Δ −0.018, p = 0.04). With a real signal, the cost pressure costs
   accuracy, and the chooser rule matters (0.377–0.421 across rules).
6. **Training vs test accuracy** (P5 §5, T5, F05, F10). See §6.

## 6. Discussion

**The decisive observation is the overfitting gap** (T5, F05).

| | Training BA | Inner-CV BA | Test BA |
|---|---|---|---|
| FBCSP all | 0.999 | 0.342 | 0.353 |
| GA single-obj. | 0.751 | 0.568 | 0.353 |
| GA-NSGA | 0.576 | 0.512 | 0.345 |
| EEGNet (32 ch, P4 recipe, seed 0) | 0.639 | (validation 0.364) | 0.350 |

All values are block protocol. Every model fits its training trials (FBCSP all
memorises them with 168 features on about 90 trials), and every model lands at the
same test accuracy, about chance + 0.02. Reducing the model to 13 features lowers
the training fit but not the test accuracy. EEGNet trained without early stopping
reaches training BA 1.0, while its validation BA stays at chance for 300 epochs
(F10). So EEGNet's near-chance accuracy is not a fault of its training recipe (P5
§5.2).

**Why the GA cannot help here.** The GA's fitness, a 3-fold inner CV on about 90
trials, is dominated by noise. Without selection that estimate is honest (FBCSP
all: inner 0.342 vs test 0.353). The GA takes the best of about 600 such estimates
and so selects luck: a +0.17 to +0.21 winner's curse, flat gene frequencies, chance-level
stability, and no advantage over random subsets of the same size. The
"stability" objective (inner-CV SD) is estimated from the same three folds and is
just as noisy, so it does not make the selection more reproducible. Selection can
only help when some components carry more class information than others, *and* the
inner CV can detect it. On the imagery data neither holds.

**When a signal is present** (viewing period), the trade-off appears as expected:
smaller models lose accuracy, and GA-NSGA's cost pressure costs 0.025 BA at 67 % less
cost. A stability-aware GA is therefore a cost-reduction tool with a measurable
accuracy price, not a way to recover a weak signal.

**What remains positive.** The pipeline is sound and reproducible: viewing decodes
well, random splits do not inflate results, and the whole analysis is bit-identical
across three machines. The GA does deliver the same (chance-level) imagery accuracy
with 8 % of the features and the smallest cross-session drop. For a low-cost device
that would matter, if the imagery signal were decodable at all.

## 7. Limitations

* **Weak signal.** Group accuracy is at most 0.02 above chance; with n = 22 the
  minimum detectable difference is about 0.024 BA. Smaller GA effects cannot be
  excluded.
* **One dataset, one task, 3 classes, 4-s imagery period, 120 trials per session.**
  The conclusions concern this regime of very few trials per class.
* **Fixed design.** The 8 candidate channels, 4 bands and 7 windows, the GA budget
  (population 30 × 20 generations) and the EEGNet recipe were frozen before the
  results. P5 shows that the chooser rule and EEGNet's early stopping are not the
  bottleneck, but other search spaces or budgets were not tried.
* **Inner CV with 3 folds** of about 30 trials each is the GA's only fitness signal.
  This is inherent to nested evaluation on this dataset size.
* **Trial-order confounds.** Block orders are reused across sessions for half of the
  subjects, and sub-01 repeats its whole sequence. The methods show no sign of
  exploiting this, but any cross-session or cross-subject analysis of this dataset
  must account for it.
* **Artifact handling.** Rejection uses only the 8 candidate channels (no EOG, no ICA),
  so EEGNet with 32 channels sees frontal blink activity in kept trials.
* **Exploratory analyses** (§5.3) were not corrected for multiplicity across checks.
  The EEGNet diagnostic uses seed 0 only, and two of its variants ran on CPU (a device
  change that acts like a different random seed; P5 §5.2).
* **Timings** (`train_time_s`, `infer_time_ms`) come from three machines and are not
  compared.

## 8. Deviations from the plan and known issues

1. **Results existed on one machine before the plan was pushed** (RESULTS.md §6.1,
   P3_REPORT_R07 §4.3). R07 ran the full grid locally on 2026-11-08 between 18:30
   and 20:12; the plan was pushed at 22:49. Those files were never committed. Every
   analysed file comes from runs made after the plan, and they are bit-identical to
   the earlier local runs (and R01/R02's files to R07's reruns), so the earlier runs
   could not have influenced any result.
2. **R07-11: `run_eegnet.py --device` changed `config_hash`** (CONTRACT_ISSUES R07-11).
   R07's first P4 pass used the flag and got a different hash with identical
   predictions. It was discarded and re-run without the flag; every committed P4 file
   has `08e124167b`. Fixed after `p4-results` (PR #16): `config_hash` now ignores
   runtime keys.
3. **R07-12: `analysis.load_results("results")` failed** on a summary JSON. P3
   analyses loaded a scratch copy of the method folders. Fixed in PR #16; outputs
   unchanged.
4. **RESULTS.md erratum** (2026-11-10): two table cells had been rounded twice (S2
   r_rb +0.14 → +0.13; S3 median Δ −0.002 → −0.001). No p-value or conclusion changes;
   T2 is generated directly from the results.
5. **Exploratory additions.** The 95 % t-interval and Friedman test in RESULTS.md, and
   all of P5. ANALYSIS_PLAN §4.6 had declared random-vs-block inflation for the GA and
   EEGNet a gap. It was filled in P5 with runner options written before the plan and
   ported unchanged (block and cross-session outputs bit-identical).
6. **One machine for P5/P6.** R01 and R02 were offline. All exploratory runs are on
   R07 and labelled `AA_HOST=R07`.
7. **P5 EEGNet per-epoch histories not retained.** The full per-fold curves (git-ignored)
   were deleted with the `p5` worktree. The committed mean curves (`curves.csv`, F10) and
   the per-fold fields in the result files hold every reported number, and re-running
   `experiments.eegnet_diag` regenerates the histories.
